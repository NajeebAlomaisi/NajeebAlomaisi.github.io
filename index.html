About

Dr. Najeeb Al-Omaisi is an academic researcher specializing in public administration, economic governance, public finance, conflict economies, institutional fragmentation, and state fragility, with a regional focus on Yemen and the wider MENA region.

His research examines how institutions, markets, elites, armed actors, external alignments, and fragmented public authorities shape governance outcomes, conflict dynamics, and peacebuilding trajectories in fragile and conflict-affected contexts.

Public Administration Political Economy Conflict Studies Economic Governance Public Finance Yemen MENA

Research Areas

Conflict Economies

Research on war economies, informal markets, taxation by armed actors, currency fragmentation, external patronage, and the political economy of peacebuilding failure.

Economic Governance

Analysis of public finance, fiscal institutions, monetary governance, state capacity, and administrative reform in fragile and divided political orders.

Institutional Fragmentation

Study of divided sovereignty, competing authorities, fragmented bureaucracies, and the governance implications of state breakdown.

Federalism and State Formation

Critical analysis of federalism, decentralization, institutional design, and public opinion in contexts of fragmentation and contested statehood.

Minority Governance

Comparative research on recognition, non-recognition, legal pluralism, mediation, and governance of religious minorities in Yemen.

Academic Freedom

Research on the impact of war on academics, institutional autonomy, scholarly displacement, and resilience in Yemen and Sudan.
